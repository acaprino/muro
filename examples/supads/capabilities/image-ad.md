# Image Ad

Turns a product into finished static ads: the real product, one message, drawn copy, in every ratio the placement needs. `$IA` below is `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills/generating-image-ads`. The packaged original, `$IA/SKILL.source.md`, is the full version of this method and wins where this body differs from it, except for the changes listed in the Sources section. Open it, and `$IA/references/ad-craft.md`, where a step says so.

## Behavior

1. **Read what you have.**
   - A product image or URL is available: run the product analysis of `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills/analyzing-products/SKILL.source.md` (its URL mode or its photo mode, with the one reference that mode names) and take four things back: what the product is; how a person physically uses it (sprayed, pumped, worn); which parts open or move, and in what order; the details that must stay identical wherever it appears.
   - Look at the image before describing it, and take the product only from what the image shows. Run `image_analysis` and ask for what this ad needs that the product facts leave out: how the surfaces take light, and whatever else the frame will have to describe.
   - No product supplied: do not guess at it. It becomes the first question of step 2.
2. **Interview, only for real gaps.** Skip it entirely when the brief already makes the ad obvious: a clear product, a clear message, nothing load-bearing missing. Otherwise ask once, in a single message, with a free-text way out; what this method already settles is not a question. Ask, when the answer is missing: the product (no image and no URL; offer to wait for an upload, a photographed product beats a described one); brand guidelines, optional (palette, art direction, what never goes in frame, the typeface, the register); the offer and the one claim (only claims the user supplies are used, as written); where it runs (placement and ratios, which decide the crop and the safe zones).
   - When the user waives questions and the product is in hand, go straight on: a single image at `1:1`, a product-hero direction and one benefit-led line drawn from the product facts. Say in a sentence which settings you took; it is a statement, not a checkpoint. A missing product image is the one thing still worth raising.
3. **Find the idea.** Read `$IA/references/ad-craft.md` first. Every ad resolves one tension between a consumer truth and a product truth: the insight (a human truth about how a person uses or feels about this kind of product), then the idea in one sentence (two sentences mean two ads; a price, discount or launch is woven into the idea, not bolted on), and only then the treatment, chosen to serve the idea. In a set each ad works from a different insight, not a different treatment of the same one. Nothing is generated here.
4. **Write the copy last**, saying only what the picture leaves unsaid, from the claims the user supplied and no others: a headline, always present; support, optional, only when the headline leaves a real question unanswered; a call to action, optional, 2–4 words, rendered as a button (a filled shape with the action inside, in the accent colour, never floating text). Do not invent a claim, a statistic, a rating or a badge to fill the layout.
5. **Write one product description** and reuse it unchanged in every prompt. With a photo going with the call, cover only what the photo cannot: the angle, the state of the moving parts, the action being performed. With no reference, carry the product in full: form; closure and moving parts, open or shut; materials and finish, surface by surface; the action, taken from the mechanic step 1 established. Do not transcribe what the label says: redrawn text comes back warped.
6. **Design the ad** to `ad-craft.md` and write down: how many (one ad, or a set: the ratio family plus any variants the brief asked for, each its own image); the ratio family, every ratio generated natively, a single `1:1` where the user named no ratio or placement; the layout (focal point, reading path, where each piece of copy lands, palette and type).
7. **Select the model.** `gpt-image-2` for a designed, text-forward layout (drawn headline, offer, callouts): short labelled segments on separate lines, every string quoted exactly. `nano-banana-pro` when the ad leads on a photoreal product or a person and carries lighter text: connected sentences in one narrative paragraph, the drawn copy quoted. `seedream-5` for a photoreal ad another model refused or keeps failing: a description leading with what matters most. A model the user named comes first. Call `list_image_models` for the accepted `aspect_ratio` values, `resolution` tiers and per-model `reference_images` limits; a wrong enum fails a billed call.
8. **Show the concept and wait.** Everything after this step costs money. In one brief message show the direction and the product (the angle, the look being held), the copy word for word, and the layout with its format and ratios. Ask whether it is right and say plainly that any of it can change. Expect edits: rewrite, show again, continue until they approve. Do not generate a frame before that. Where the user has said they do not want to be asked, say in one line what you are going with and carry on.
9. **Generate.** Assemble each prompt as "How the prompt is assembled" in `ad-craft.md` gives it (preservation block, layout, copy, look, exclusions; under 350 words; a short genre tag at the end). The source orders its image skill read before any image call: open the chosen model's guide under `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills/generating-images/references/` (`prompt-gpt-image-2.md`, `prompt-nano-banana.md` or `prompt-seedream.md`) for how that model wants a prompt; this method keeps the model choice and the prompt's content. Then call `image_generate` once, with all requests in the same call:
   - one object per deliverable (one per ratio in the family, one per variant asked for), each with `prompt`, `model`, `aspect_ratio`, `resolution` at `1k` unless the user asked for a higher tier, and `reference_images`;
   - the product photo(s) in `reference_images` every time: the photo holds identity, the prompt alone will not;
   - one deliverable per request: never a second to compare models, try another look or correct an output you judged poor;
   - no vision or analysis call on the image just generated; hand the result to the user;
   - an unanswered question is not a yes: when you ask whether to regenerate and no answer comes, deliver what you have and say what you would have changed;
   - a result `{status: "pending", …}` is a job handle: pass that exact handle to `job_status`, again if still pending, and never re-run it through `image_generate`.
10. **Return** the image URLs and local file paths once every frame has finished, never a partial set; for a set, in the order you outlined.

For a mapped step, submit it through the coordinator's `record` command with the step's `map_id` and `capability`. ## Specifics

- Never regenerate a deliverable without the user asking: every re-run is billed again. Hand back what came out, say what looks wrong, and wait. This holds in every case below.
- Limits `ad-craft.md` sets for every ad: one product, one value proposition, one call to action; at most three focal points (headline, product, call to action); two typefaces at most; three to five colours, the brand colour as an accent held to a tenth of the frame; up to three benefit callouts; one primary light, named with quality and direction; on a full-screen vertical the top strip and the bottom third are covered by the platform, so drawn copy and the offer stay out of them; with several references, Image 1 is always the product. Open its sections "The ratio family and safe zones", "Anti-patterns" and "Realism — the reject filters" when designing and when judging what to say about a result.
- The image came back not as intended (warped text, a look not asked for): deliver it and say what is off. For warped or misspelled text the fix to propose is to quote the copy exactly, cut the amount, raise the resolution and route to `gpt-image-2`.
- One deliverable fails while the others succeed: send a new call with only that request. A rejection that names no reason: ask what missed, with options drawn from the direction you built plus free text, before any re-run.
- Safety or policy rejection: name a workable stand-in and resubmit; on a second, try another model from step 7; on a third, say which element is blocked.
- `reference_images` rejected on count: the error states the limit; drop to it. `error: "no_provider_configured"` on any tool: relay the tool's `hint` (the user must set their key).
- A failure this list does not cover (a call that times out with no handle, an explicit error that names no cause): open "Edge cases" in `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills/generating-images/SKILL.source.md`. Where both speak, this method's own rule wins.
- The user changes something after approving: redo from the earliest step the change touches. A different direction or layout is a new design; a different line is only the copy and the prompt; a different ratio is only that object.
- No product supplied and the user wants to proceed anyway: ask for the product first. "Common mistakes" in the original lists what a design is checked against.
- Tool limits: at most 10 requests per call and 10 reference images per request; `4:5` and `21:9` are accepted by some models only. The tool has no output folder argument: files go to the server's `SUPERCMO_OUTPUT_DIR` folder, else `./supercmo-media` under the server's working directory; report the `path` each result gives.

## Checks

- **concept_approved_before_generating**: the direction, the copy word for word and the layout with its ratios were shown and approved before the first `image_generate` call, or stated in one line when the user had said not to be asked.
- **only_supplied_claims_drawn**: every drawn string comes from what the user supplied (or, with questions waived, is one benefit-led line from the product facts); no claim, statistic, rating or badge was invented.
- **product_reference_on_every_request**: the product photo is in `reference_images` of every request and each prompt states that the product is the one in the reference, unchanged.
- **no_unrequested_regeneration**: one request per deliverable; nothing was generated a second time without the user asking, no analysis call was run on a generated image, and pending handles were rejoined through `job_status`.
