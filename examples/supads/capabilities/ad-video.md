# Product Ad Video

Turns a product into a finished commercial video. One flow with two optional stages: a voiceover (on by default) and an actor (only where a person is on camera). The packaged original, `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills/generating-ad-videos/SKILL.source.md`, is the full version of this method and wins where this body differs from it, except for the changes listed in the Sources section. `$S` below is `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills`. Open a packaged file where a step says so.

The ad runs on `seedance-2.0-fast` unless the user named a model, and is delivered `9:16` unless the user explicitly asked for another ratio.

## Behavior

1. **Read what you have.** For a product image or URL, open `$S/analyzing-products/SKILL.source.md` and follow it: what the product is, how a person uses it, which parts open or move, what must stay identical. Run `image_analysis` on every supplied image, not only the product. What each image is for comes from the brief, not from its contents. With no product, do not guess one: it is the first thing step 2 asks for.
2. **Interview.** Skip it when the brief already settles the ad: a clear product, a clear intent, a length. Otherwise ask once, in a single message, always with a free-text way out:
   - the product, a link or a photo, when neither was supplied (offer to wait for an upload);
   - how long, when the brief does not say: offer 15s, 30s, 45s or 60s, or their own;
   - whether a presenter or actor is on camera, when the brief does not say;
   - whether a voiceover carries it, when the brief does not say. The default is yes; they can pick music and effects only. Any words are a voiceover, never spoken on camera.
   - When the user waives questions, take 15s, no actor and a voiceover on, say in one sentence what you took and keep going.
3. **Write the product description** once and reuse it unchanged in every storyboard: material and finish surface by surface; the real width and height and how it sits against a hand; two to five visual anchors you can verify in the product image; the mechanism (the parts that move, how they open or close, where anything dispenses); what may be done with each part. Nothing later does anything to the product that is not on that list. Do not write out what the label says, even where the product facts spell it out.
4. **Plan the concept.** Follow the brief exactly wherever it is specific.
   - Read `$S/generating-ad-videos/references/commercial-craft.md` end to end. It governs every choice from here to the final frame: the angle, the register, the product-identity lock, on-screen text, the screen-lock, wardrobe, the shots an ad never uses, the packshot and the sound.
   - Choose the angle the ad sells on, and with it whether anyone is on camera. Fix the register and the one setting (a surface, a light, a palette) and hold them across the whole ad. Only where a person is on camera, decide the one presenter: age, look and demeanour.
   - Split the length into clips. `list_video_models` gives the clip model's shortest and longest clip. Make each clip as long as the model allows and let the leftover be the last one; where the leftover falls under the minimum, give the last clip the minimum and let the ad run slightly over. On a 4–15s model: 30s is two clips of 15; 50s is 15, 15, 15 and 5; 18s is 15 and 4.
   - Lay the arc across the clips, shot by shot, and close on the packshot. Never run two shots of the same scale back to back. Where the ad is too short to hold every shot, carry fewer. With no one on screen, place one signature shot around the middle. The shot lists for both cases are in step 4f of the original.
   - Write it down: the register and setting, the presenter where there is one, how many clips and how many seconds each, the shot each clip carries, and one line of voiceover per clip where the ad speaks.
5. **Write the voiceover, where the ad has one.** Open `$S/writing-video-scripts/SKILL.source.md` with what the voiceover should communicate, the concept, and a word budget of two words a second as the target and three the ceiling. It is heard as a voiceover over the picture, and it uses only the claims the user supplied, as written. It returns one segment per clip. With no words, skip this stage.
6. **Show the concept and wait.** Nothing has been generated yet, and everything after this step costs money. In one brief message show the presenter (only where a person is on camera), the product and the look being held, what is on screen in each clip, and the words of each clip (only where the ad speaks). Say they can change any of it. Expect edits and show it again until they approve. Do not cast, build a sheet or generate a clip before the answer. Where the user said not to ask, say in one line what you are going with and carry on.
7. **Cast the presenter, where a person is on camera.** Only once the concept is approved. Skip it where no one is on camera or the user supplied a photo of the presenter. Otherwise open `$S/generating-ai-actors/SKILL.source.md` with the presenter from the approved concept, wardrobe whose accent picks up or cleanly contrasts the product's colour, and the register. Cast once and reuse the image everywhere.
8. **Build the storyboards.** Open `$S/generating-storyboards/SKILL.source.md`, one sheet per clip, with what happens in the clip and its seconds, which sheet this is of how many, the product image (and the presenter's), the product description unchanged, the clip's script segment, and the register and setting as the look. A sheet is always `16:9` with vertical panels, whatever ratio the ad delivers in. Show all sheets in one message and wait; ask the user to check the product in every panel. Rebuild only the sheet a changed panel is on.
9. **Write the prompts.** Open `$S/writing-video-prompts/SKILL.source.md`: one prompt per clip, with the model, the clip's duration, the panels of its sheet, the product description unchanged, the script segment, and the media (the sheet, the product image, the presenter's image). Spoken lines are voiceover over the picture, never lip-synced. It returns the prompts and the order it labelled the media in.
10. **Generate.** One `video_generate` call with one request per clip, so they render at once. Per request: the prompt; the labelled media as `reference_images` in that order; `duration`, `resolution`, `aspect_ratio`; `generate_audio` on. Omit `model` to use the default. Every clip uses the same aspect ratio and resolution. A clip that comes back as `{status: "pending", …}` is a job handle, not a failure: pass that exact handle to `job_status`, and again if it is still pending. Never re-run a pending clip; that starts a second billed job.
11. **Stitch and return.** When every clip has finished, pass them in order to `video_stitch`; where the user wanted a music bed over the whole ad, pass it as `music`. Do not join them by hand. A single clip needs no stitching. Return the finished ad's URL and local file path, and the single clips if the user asks.

## Output handoff

Resolve `<package_root>` with the `package_root` returned beside these instructions, never against the consumer project. Return `ad_video` as one JSON string: the finished ad's URL and local path, the clips in order with their paths, the approved concept (angle, register, setting, presenter, clip split), the product description, the voiceover text per clip, the model and ratio used, any clip still pending with its job handle, and what failed or was refused. For a mapped step, submit it through the coordinator's `record` command with the step's `map_id` and `capability`. Do not edit `<state_dir>/map.md` or `<state_dir>/state.json`; the coordinator is their only writer.

## Specifics

- Never regenerate a clip without the user asking: every re-run is billed again. Hand back what came out, say what looks wrong and wait. One clip of a batch fails: send a new call with only that request. A wrong sheet is remade alone. A clip that does not match its sheet is handed over with what looks off; whether it is close enough is the user's call.
- No product supplied and the user wants to proceed anyway: ask for the product first. A safety filter rejects the presenter twice: say which element is blocked so they can be recast. A change after approval: redo from the earliest step it touches (a presenter or setting means new sheets and clips; a shot means that clip's sheet and prompt; a line means the script and that clip's prompt). `no_provider_configured` on any tool: relay the tool's `hint`.
- The source orders `$S/generating-videos/SKILL.source.md` read before `video_generate` is called. Here the model, the prompts and the call are settled by the steps above; open its "Edge cases" when a clip is refused, fails or times out in a way this body does not cover.
- Where files land: the server saves every sheet, image and clip into its media output folder, which is the folder named by `SUPERCMO_OUTPUT_DIR` in the server's environment, else `./supercmo-media` under the server's working directory. `video_generate` and `image_generate` take no output folder argument. `video_stitch` takes `output`, a file path; without it the file is `stitched_<N>clips.mp4` in the same folder. Give the user the `path` each tool returns.
- Runtime dependency: `ffmpeg` on the PATH, for `video_stitch` only. The tool checks for it and answers `ffmpeg is not installed or not on PATH.` with the hint `brew install ffmpeg` / `apt-get install ffmpeg`; with `dry_run: true` it runs the same check and joins nothing. The source gives no install method for Windows. Where `video_stitch` cannot run, return the clips in order and say they are not joined.

## Checks

- **real_product_not_invented**: the ad was built from a product image or URL the user supplied, the one product description was reused unchanged, and no product, variant or feature was invented.
- **approved_before_spend**: the concept, and then every sheet, were shown and approved before any clip was generated; or the user said not to ask and each choice was stated in one line.
- **no_unasked_rerun**: no pending clip was resubmitted, and no clip was generated a second time without the user asking.
- **finished_ad_returned**: the finished ad's URL and local file path were returned; an ad of several clips is one file joined by `video_stitch`, or the clips in order with the statement that they are not joined.
