# UGC Video

Turns a product into a video of a creator on camera. The packaged original, `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills/generating-ugc-videos/SKILL.source.md`, is the full version of this method and wins where this body differs from it, except for the changes listed in the Sources section. `$S` below is `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills`. Open a packaged file where a step says so.

Settled for every job, never asked and never re-decided: the video is delivered `9:16` unless the user explicitly asked for `16:9`; the clip model is `seedance-2.0-fast` with audio on, unless the user named one; storyboard sheets are `gpt-image-2` at `16:9`; the number of clips comes from the length; the clips are always joined, with hard cuts and no transitions.

## Behavior

1. **Read what you have.** For a product image or URL, open `$S/analyzing-products/SKILL.source.md` and follow it: what the product is, how a person uses it, which parts open or move, what must stay identical. Run `image_analysis` on every supplied image, not only the product. What each image is for comes from the brief, not from its contents: a person in a frame does not make it a casting photo. With no product, do not guess one: it is the first thing step 2 asks for.
2. **Interview.** Skip it when the brief already settles the video: a clear product, a clear format, a length. Otherwise ask once, in a single message, always with a free-text way out:
   - the product, a link or a photo, when neither was supplied (offer to wait for an upload);
   - how long, when the brief does not say: offer 15s, 30s, 45s or 60s, or their own;
   - whether the person in a supplied photo should be the creator, when an image with a person was supplied and the brief does not say who they are;
   - whether they have a photo of the delivery package, when the brief is about opening a package and none is attached. If they have one, wait for it to arrive before building anything. If they do not, a plain unbranded box stands in.
   - When the user waives questions, take 15s, say in one sentence what you took and keep going.
3. **Select the mode and read its guide** in `$S/generating-ugc-videos/references/`: holding the product and talking about it → review, `mode-review.md`; opening the package it arrived in → unboxing, `mode-unboxing.md`; wearing it → try-on, `mode-try-on.md`; showing how to use it one step at a time → tutorial, `mode-tutorial.md`. Where two fit: a sealed package opened on camera → unboxing; the product ends up worn → try-on, even when a bag is opened to reach it; numbered steps or "how do I use this" → tutorial; nothing to open, wear or work through → review. The guide carries the arc: what the video does, in what order, what the panels hold and how it ends.
4. **Write the product description** once and reuse it unchanged in every storyboard: material and finish surface by surface; the real width and height and how it sits against a hand; two to five visual anchors you can verify in the product image; the mechanism (the parts that move, how they open or close, where anything dispenses); what may be done with each part. Nothing later does anything to the product that is not on that list. Do not write out what the label says, even where the product facts spell it out.
5. **Write the concept.** What the user asked for wins: follow the brief exactly wherever it is specific. Everything else is yours to decide.
   - Who the creator is and where: as the brief names them, else whoever the video is most believable coming from, where it would actually happen.
   - How many clips: one storyboard becomes one clip. Read the clip model's shortest and longest clip from `list_video_models`. Make each clip as long as the model allows and let the leftover be the last one; where the leftover falls under the minimum, give the last clip the minimum and let the video run slightly over. On a 4–15s model: 30s is two clips of 15; 50s is 15, 15, 15 and 5; 18s is 15 and 4.
   - What the story can hold: a primary action is one continuous motion at one object and needs two seconds where possible, three at most, so a clip's length is the number of primary actions it carries. These never render: anything finer than a whole hand movement (a clasp, a zip, a drawstring, a pump head); two motions treated as one, or a second object handled in the same breath; a change of state shown on screen (the panel arrives with the product already open, worn or empty); going back (nothing later reseals, removes or restores it).
   - Write it down: who the creator is, how many clips and how many seconds each, what happens in each clip following the mode guide's arc, and roughly how many words each clip can carry.
6. **Write the script.** Open `$S/writing-video-scripts/SKILL.source.md` with what the creator should communicate and the concept (clip durations, beats, word budget). The words are spoken on camera and lip-synced. Only the claims the user supplied, used as written; none supplied means none made.
7. **Show the concept and wait.** Nothing has been generated yet, and everything after this step costs money. In one brief message show the creator (who they are, age, ethnicity, look, clothes, place), what is on screen in each clip, and the script of each clip word for word. Say they can change any of it. Expect edits and show it again until they approve. Do not cast, build a sheet or generate a clip before the answer. Where the user said not to ask, say in one line what you are going with and carry on.
8. **Cast the creator.** Where the user already settled who that is (named in the brief, or confirmed in step 2), their supplied photo is the creator image: skip this step. Otherwise open `$S/generating-ai-actors/SKILL.source.md` with: the person from the approved concept, not a fresh invention; empty hands; a phone self-portrait in available light, not a studio portrait; `nano-banana-pro` at `3:4`. Keep the result: it is the identity reference for every storyboard and clip.
9. **Build the storyboards.** Open `$S/generating-storyboards/SKILL.source.md`, one sheet per clip, with what happens in the clip and its seconds, which sheet this is of how many, the product image or images and the creator image as references, the clip's script segment, the product description unchanged, and the look: a phone photograph in available light, sensor grain and real skin, no studio lighting. A sheet is always `16:9` with vertical panels. Show all sheets in one message and wait; ask the user to check the product in every panel. Rebuild only the sheet a changed panel is on.
10. **Write the clip prompts.** Open `$S/writing-video-prompts/SKILL.source.md`: one prompt per storyboard, one cut per panel of that sheet, with the clip model with audio on, the clip's duration, the delivery ratio, the sheet's panels, the script segment, the product description unchanged, and the images (the storyboard, the creator image, the product image or images).
11. **Generate.** One `video_generate` call with one request per storyboard, so they render at once. Per request: the prompt, `model`, `duration`, `generate_audio` on, `aspect_ratio` the delivery ratio (the same on every clip), and `reference_images`: that storyboard, the creator image and the product image or images, in the order the prompt labelled them. Everything goes in as a reference and nothing as a start frame. Never generate a second version to compare takes. Read accepted values from `list_video_models`: a wrong value fails a billed call. A clip that comes back as `{status: "pending", …}` is a job handle: pass it to `job_status`, and again if it is still pending. Never re-run a pending clip.
12. **Stitch and return.** When every clip has finished, join them in order with `video_stitch`. A single clip is already the video. Hand over the finished video. Do not present storyboards, separate clips or job handles unless asked.

## Output handoff

Resolve `<package_root>` with the `package_root` returned beside these instructions, never against the consumer project. Return `ugc_video` as one JSON string: the finished video's URL and local path, the mode, the approved concept (creator, clip split, what happens in each clip), the script per clip, the product description, the creator image path, the model and ratio used, any clip still pending with its job handle, and what failed, was refused or could not run. For a mapped step, submit it through the coordinator's `record` command with the step's `map_id` and `capability`. Do not edit `<state_dir>/map.md` or `<state_dir>/state.json`; the coordinator is their only writer.

## Specifics

- A generation refused as unsafe is usually the creator: regenerate the creator in more covering clothing, then rebuild the storyboards that used it. Identical inputs fail identically, so change something. After a second refusal, name the blocked element.
- A clip refused because a reference image may show a real person: neither the wardrobe nor the prompt changes it. Recast the creator as a different person, rebuild every storyboard that used the old one and resubmit. If it happens again, switch the clip model; this is the one thing that reopens the settled clip model.
- A product URL that will not load: ask for a photo. A clip that comes back wrong: deliver it and say what you think is off. `reference_images` rejected on count: the error states the limit; drop to it. `no_provider_configured`: relay the tool's `hint`. Where the product analysis or `video_stitch` cannot run, do the rest and say which step could not run.
- The source orders `$S/generating-videos/SKILL.source.md` read before `video_generate` is called. Here the model, the prompts and the call are settled by the steps above; open its "Edge cases" when a clip fails or times out in a way this body does not cover.
- Where files land: the server saves every sheet, image and clip into its media output folder, which is the folder named by `SUPERCMO_OUTPUT_DIR` in the server's environment, else `./supercmo-media` under the server's working directory. `video_generate` and `image_generate` take no output folder argument. `video_stitch` takes `output`, a file path; without it the file is `stitched_<N>clips.mp4` in the same folder. Give the user the `path` the tool returns.
- Runtime dependency: `ffmpeg` on the PATH, for `video_stitch` only. The tool checks for it and answers `ffmpeg is not installed or not on PATH.` with the hint `brew install ffmpeg` / `apt-get install ffmpeg`; with `dry_run: true` it runs the same check and joins nothing. The source gives no install method for Windows.

## Checks

- **approved_before_spend**: the concept, and then every sheet, were shown and approved before the creator was cast or any clip was generated; or the user said not to ask and each choice was stated in one line.
- **claims_only_as_supplied**: the script makes only the claims the user supplied, as written; with none supplied it makes none.
- **references_never_start_frame**: every clip received its storyboard, the creator image and the product image or images as `reference_images` in the labelled order, nothing as a start frame, and the same delivery ratio.
- **finished_video_returned**: one finished video was handed over, joined by `video_stitch` when there are several clips, with no pending clip resubmitted and no second version generated to compare takes.
