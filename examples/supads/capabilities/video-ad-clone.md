# Video Ad Clone

Takes a video ad the user likes and rebuilds it with the user's own product. The reference ad's structure, pacing, shots and hook are kept; its product, brand and spoken lines are replaced. The packaged original, `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills/cloning-video-ads/SKILL.source.md`, is the full version of this method and wins where this body differs from it, except for the changes listed in the Sources section. `$S` below is `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills` and `$R` is `$S/cloning-video-ads/references`. Open a packaged file where a step says so.

## Behavior

1. **Analyze the reference ad.** The reference ad is the plan the clone is rebuilt from: read it first and in full, because no later step recovers a thin read. Read `$R/reading-the-ad.md` for what to pull out and the breakdown format, then run `video_analysis` on the ad (a video file or URL) and ask for that timed, second-by-second breakdown: one line naming the ad's style and format, one line per shot tagged `[start-end]`, one line naming the production qualities. The spans are contiguous and sum to the ad's length; the number of shots is the number of real cuts. With no reference ad, do not invent one: it is the first thing step 3 asks for.
2. **Read the product.** For the user's product image or URL, open `$S/analyzing-products/SKILL.source.md` and follow it: what the product is, how a person uses it, which parts open or move, what must stay identical. Run `image_analysis` on every other supplied image. With no product, do not guess one.
3. **Interview.** Skip it when you already have both the reference ad and the product, and the user has not asked to change the length or the ratio. Otherwise ask once, in a single message, always with a free-text way out:
   - the reference ad, when it was not supplied (offer to wait for an upload or a link);
   - the product, a link or a photo, when neither was supplied;
   - how long, only if the user wants a length other than the reference ad's;
   - the aspect ratio, only if the user wants a ratio other than the reference ad's.
   - When the user waives questions, match the reference ad's length and ratio, say in one sentence what you took and keep going.
4. **Write the product description** once and reuse it unchanged in the clip prompt: material and finish surface by surface; the real width and height and how it sits against a hand; two to five visual anchors you can verify in the product image; the mechanism (the parts that move, how they open or close, where anything dispenses); what may be done with each part. Nothing later does anything to the product that is not on that list. Do not write out what the label says, even where the product facts spell it out.
5. **Build the clone blueprint.** Read `$R/rebuilding-the-ad.md`. Turn the read of step 1 into a shot-by-shot blueprint that keeps the form (shots, order, timing, camera, pacing, light and colour, on-screen-text style, audio register, arc) and swaps only the identity (product, spoken words, brand, the hand-interaction re-fitted to how the user's product works, the people recast for the user's product and buyer).
   - Keep the whole ad in one clip. The blueprint's shots are the cuts inside a single continuous clip.
   - Pick the clip model and let it cap the length. Read each model's shortest and longest clip from `list_video_models`: up to about 15 seconds runs on `seedance-2.0-fast`, longer up to 30 seconds on `seedance-2.5`. If the target is longer than the longest clip available, cap it there and say so.
   - Fit it to that length. Shorter than the reference: drop the most redundant cuts and keep the beats that define the ad. Longer: hold the most expressive beats a little longer. The spans stay contiguous and sum to the clone's length. Same length: keep the timing shot for shot.
   - Write it down: for each shot its seconds, what is on screen, the camera move, where the product sits and what is done to it, and one line of what is said where the ad speaks.
6. **Rewrite the voiceover, where the reference ad speaks.** Open `$S/writing-video-scripts/SKILL.source.md` with what the voiceover should communicate, the blueprint, and the reference ad's own spoken lines as the structure to match, at the same length and cadence. It is heard as a voiceover over the picture, never lip-synced. Use only the claims the user supplied and drop the original brand's; where the original made a claim the user has not supplied, do not carry it over and do not invent one. Where the reference ad has no spoken words, skip this stage.
7. **Show the blueprint and wait.** Nothing has been generated yet, and everything after this step costs money. In one brief message show what is kept from the reference ad in a sentence, the user's product and how it stands in for the original's, what is on screen in each shot with its camera move, and the rewritten words (only where the ad speaks). Say they can change any of it. Expect edits and show it again until they approve. Do not write a prompt or generate a clip before the answer. Where the user said not to ask, say in one line what you are going with and carry on.
8. **Build the storyboard.** Only once the blueprint is approved. The sheet is a reference that holds the product identical, not a frame-by-frame plan: it covers the key moments only, at most five panels. Pick the beats that most need the product locked (the hook, the shots where the product is featured or handled, the close) and leave the finer cuts to the clip prompt. Open `$S/generating-storyboards/SKILL.source.md`, one sheet for the clip, with those beats and their seconds, the product image or images, the product description unchanged, the look and register carried from the reference ad, and the script segment where the ad speaks. The sheet is always `16:9`, with the panels carrying the clone's delivery ratio. Show the sheet and wait; ask the user to check the product in every panel, and rebuild the sheet until they approve.
9. **Write the prompt.** Open `$S/writing-video-prompts/SKILL.source.md`: one prompt for the whole clip, with the model and length from step 5, the panels of the sheet as the cuts inside the clip (one cut per panel), the product description unchanged, the rewritten script where the ad speaks, and the media (the storyboard sheet and the product image or images) labelled as `reference_images`. Spoken lines are voiceover over the picture, never lip-synced.
10. **Generate.** Call `video_generate` with a single request: the prompt; the `model` from step 5; the sheet and the product image or images as `reference_images` in the labelled order; `duration`, `resolution`, `aspect_ratio`; `generate_audio` on, so the clone carries the reference ad's sound. A clip that comes back as `{status: "pending", …}` is a job handle: pass that exact handle to `job_status`, and again if it is still pending. Never re-run a pending clip; that starts a second billed job.
11. **Return** the finished clone's URL and local file path.

## Specifics

- A clone copies craft, not content. It never reproduces the reference ad's own footage, its brand name, logo, wordmark or slogan, its actual music track, or the likeness of the people in it. The only brand shown is the user's, and only where the reference showed one. If a shot cannot be described without naming or showing the original brand, describe what the shot does and let the user's product fill it. The rules for holding the product true, on-screen text and sound are in `$R/rebuilding-the-ad.md`.
- Never regenerate a clip without the user asking: every re-run is billed again. Hand back what came out, say what looks wrong and wait. A clone that does not match the reference's structure, or a clip that does not match its sheet, is handed over with what drifted; whether it is close enough is the user's call. A wrong sheet is remade before generating.
- No reference ad: there is nothing to clone (an ad built from scratch is the capability `ad-video`). No product and the user wants to proceed anyway: ask for the product first. A reference ad too long or too large for `video_analysis`, which reads the clip inline: ask for a shorter ad, a trimmed section or a link to one. A safety filter rejects the clip: say what is likely being caught (often a person or the original brand's mark) so it can be reworded. `no_provider_configured` on any tool: relay the tool's `hint`.
- The source orders `$S/generating-videos/SKILL.source.md` read before `video_generate` is called. Here the model, the prompt and the call are settled by the steps above; open its "Edge cases" when the clip fails or times out in a way this body does not cover.
- Where files land: the server saves the sheet and the clip into its media output folder, which is the folder named by `SUPERCMO_OUTPUT_DIR` in the server's environment, else `./supercmo-media` under the server's working directory. `video_generate` and `image_generate` take no output folder argument. Give the user the `path` the tool returns.
- Runtime dependency: provider keys only (below). This capability calls no tool that needs `ffmpeg`: the clone is one clip and is not stitched.

## Checks

- **reference_read_as_timed_breakdown**: the reference ad was read with `video_analysis` into a timed breakdown (a style line, one line per shot with its `[start-end]` span, a production line) whose spans are contiguous and sum to the ad's length.
- **blueprint_approved_before_spend**: the blueprint was shown and approved before the sheet was built, and the sheet before the clip was generated; or the user said not to ask and each choice was stated in one line.
- **original_identity_replaced**: the blueprint and the prompt carry none of the reference ad's footage, brand name, logo, wordmark, slogan, music track or people's likeness, and the spoken lines make only the claims the user supplied.
- **clone_url_and_path_returned**: one finished clip was returned with its URL and local file path, with no pending clip resubmitted and no clip generated a second time without the user asking.
