# Product Photos

Turns a product into commercial imagery through `image_generate`, in one of ten shot modes. `$PP` below is `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills/generating-product-photos`. The packaged original, `$PP/SKILL.source.md`, is the full version of this method and wins where this body differs from it, except for the changes listed in the Sources section. Each mode has a guide under `$PP/references/`; `$PP/references/photographic-craft.md` is read on every job.

## Behavior

1. **Read what you have.**
   - A product image or URL is available: run the product analysis of `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills/analyzing-products/SKILL.source.md` (its URL mode or its photo mode, with the one reference that mode names) and take four things back: what the product is; how a person physically uses it (sprayed, pumped, worn); which parts open or move, and in what order; the details that must stay identical wherever it appears.
   - Look at the image before describing it. Take the product only from what the image shows: not the brief, not the filename, not the product name. Run `image_analysis` and ask for what this shoot needs that the product facts leave out: how the surfaces take light, and whatever else the frame will have to describe.
   - No product supplied: do not guess at it. It becomes the first question of step 2.
2. **Interview, only for real gaps.** Skip it entirely when the brief already makes the shot obvious: a clear subject, a clear use, nothing load-bearing missing. Otherwise ask once, in a single message, at most four questions, with a free-text way out; what this method already settles is not a question. Ask, when the answer is missing: the product (no image and no URL; offer to wait for an upload); brand guidelines, optional; who the buyer is, when the frame has to speak to a particular buyer (which features an infographic calls out, whose home a lifestyle scene is set in, who is cast on-model); how many frames and where they run.
   - When the user waives questions and the product is in hand, go straight on: take **studio** and the tool's default ratio, and say in a sentence which settings you took. A missing product image is the one thing still worth raising.
3. **Select the mode and read its guide.** Route on what the image is for, not on a matched word.
   - The product alone against a clean, controlled background: **studio**, `mode-studio.md`. Somewhere real, being used or lived with: **lifestyle**, `mode-lifestyle.md`. One polished frame leading a campaign: **hero**, `mode-hero.md`. Worn, held or applied to skin: **on-model**, `mode-on-model.md`. Texture, material, craft, a tight crop: **close-up**, `mode-close-up.md`. Arranged on a surface and shot from directly above: **flat-lay**, `mode-flat-lay.md`. Tied to a holiday, season or buying moment: **seasonal**, `mode-seasonal.md`. Feature labels, callouts, specs or dimensions drawn onto the image, or an informational before-and-after or comparison: **infographic**, `mode-infographic.md`. Breaking the pattern to stop a scroll: **creative**, `mode-creative.md`. Suspended mid-air: **floating**, `mode-floating.md`.
   - Where two fit, these win: a person in frame is on-model, whatever the setting or crop; text drawn onto the image is infographic; one plane shot from overhead is flat-lay; one property at close range is close-up, even inside a setting; the occasion as the sell is seasonal; a brief asking for something unexpected, surprising or scroll-stopping is creative, even when it also names a placement.
4. **Write one product description** and reuse it unchanged in every prompt. With a photo going with the call, cover only what the photo cannot: the angle, the state of the moving parts, the action being performed. With no reference, carry the product in full: form; closure and moving parts, open or shut; materials and finish, surface by surface; the action, taken from the mechanic step 1 established. The mode guide decides how much of this the prompt carries. Do not transcribe what the label says.
5. **Select the model.** `nano-banana-pro` for everything, by default: connected sentences in one narrative paragraph, never a string of keywords. `gpt-image-2` when words or graphics are drawn onto the image or the layout is a designed one, not for the product's own printed label, which comes from the reference: short labelled segments on separate lines. `seedream-5` when another model refused or keeps failing: a description leading with what matters most. A model the user named comes first.
6. **Write the prompt.** Read `photographic-craft.md` before writing: the language for light, shadow, colour, material and optics, the style anchor to pick before the first sentence, and the two fixed blocks.
   - Assemble every prompt in this order: the mode's fixed preservation block, the scene written from the mode's fields, the quality-marker line, the Avoid block; the last two exactly as `photographic-craft.md` gives them.
   - A prompt sent with a reference photo states the fidelity requirement: the product in frame is the one in the reference and reaches the image unchanged. Do not write out what the photo already settles; those words go to the setting, the light, the framing.
   - With more than one reference image, label them Image 1, Image 2 in the order passed, say what each is, and use those numbers.
   - Scene-specific exclusions go inline, phrased as the thing you want; the closing Avoid block is fixed, add nothing of this frame to it. Do not reserve empty space for text nobody asked for. A name is not a description: no photographer, publication or rival named, only the brand of the product being photographed. No internal name (a mode, a file) goes in the prompt.
   - The source orders its image skill read before any image call: open the chosen model's guide under `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills/generating-images/references/` (`prompt-nano-banana.md`, `prompt-gpt-image-2.md` or `prompt-seedream.md`) for how that model wants a prompt; this method keeps the model choice and the prompt's content.
7. **Generate.** Call `image_generate` with a `requests` list, one object per image: `prompt`, `model`, `aspect_ratio`, `resolution` at `1k` unless the user asked for a higher tier, `reference_images`.
   - The product photo goes in `reference_images` every time.
   - Take the ratio from the user. If none was named, derive it from where the image runs: the destination decides the crop, not the mode. If neither is known, ask in step 2 or take the tool's default and say which you took.
   - One image per request, and a single frame unless the brief asked for more; several asked-for frames go as several request objects in one call. Never a second image to compare models, try another look or correct an output you judged poor.
   - No vision or analysis call on the image just generated. An unanswered question is not a yes: with no answer on whether to regenerate, deliver what you have and say what you would have changed.
   - Call `list_image_models` for the accepted `aspect_ratio` values, `resolution` tiers and per-model `reference_images` limits; a wrong enum fails a billed call.
   - A result `{status: "pending", …}` is a job handle: pass that exact handle to `job_status`, again if still pending, and never re-run it through `image_generate`.
8. **Return** the image URLs and local file paths once every frame has finished, never a partial set; for a set, in the order you outlined.

## Output handoff

Return `product_photos` as one JSON string: for each frame its mode, ratio, model, hosted URL and local file path, the settings you took by default, what you think is off in any frame and any request that failed. For a mapped step, submit it through the coordinator's `record` command with the step's `map_id` and `capability`. Do not edit `<state_dir>/map.md` or `<state_dir>/state.json`; the coordinator is their only writer.

## Specifics

- A set: requests generate independently, so follow "Producing a set of images" in the original. Draft the frame list and get it agreed before generating anything; fix one look and write it into every prompt unchanged; send the same reference image and the same model on every frame; vary only what each frame shows (a frame that needs a different setting is a second set); make the frames genuinely different; for a sequence, decide the order first.
- Limits the guides set: the written scene is 150–250 words in every mode except infographic, which is labelled lines with every string quoted and eight labels at most; one sentence per light source, two sources at most; colour temperature in Kelvin; flat-lay keeps between a third and two thirds of the frame as open surface; floating takes at most 5 unique companion element types; the environment uses two or three dominant tones.
- The product's state ("The product's state" in `photographic-craft.md`): the product leaves in the state it arrived in. Never write an action that opens, empties or takes it apart unless the user asks for that change outright; "show it open" is such an instruction, "show it being used" is not.
- The style anchor ("Photographic & Art Direction" in `photographic-craft.md`): two distinct photographers are chosen silently and their names never enter the prompt.
- The image came back not as intended (wrong angle, warped label, a look not asked for): deliver it and say what is off; do not regenerate on your own judgement. A rejection that names no reason: ask what missed, with options drawn from the mode you shot plus free text, before any re-run.
- Safety or policy rejection: name a workable stand-in and resubmit; on a second, try `seedream-5`; on a third, say which element is blocked. Generic failure: when the error names a parameter or limit, fix that and resubmit; otherwise resubmit once, then hand the user the error text.
- `reference_images` rejected on count: the error states the limit; drop to it. `error: "no_provider_configured"`: relay the tool's `hint` (the user must set their key).
- A call that times out with no handle: open "Edge cases" in `<package_root>/scripts/supercmo-skills/skills/generating-images/SKILL.source.md`. Where both speak, this method's own rule wins. "Common mistakes" in this method's original lists what a prompt is checked against.
- Tool limits: at most 10 requests per call and 10 reference images per request; the default ratio is `1:1`; `4:5` and `21:9` are accepted by some models only. The tool has no output folder argument: files go to the server's `SUPERCMO_OUTPUT_DIR` folder, else `./supercmo-media` under the server's working directory; report the `path` each result gives.

## Checks

- **mode_guide_and_fixed_blocks_used**: one mode was selected and its guide read, and each prompt is the mode's preservation block (when a reference is attached), the scene, then the quality-marker line and the Avoid block as `photographic-craft.md` gives them.
- **product_reference_on_every_request**: the product photo is in `reference_images` of every request and each prompt states that the product is the one in the reference, unchanged.
- **frame_list_agreed_for_sets**: the frame list was drafted and agreed before the first image of the set was generated.
- **no_unrequested_regeneration**: one image per request; nothing was generated a second time without the user asking, no analysis call was run on a generated image, and pending handles were rejoined through `job_status`.
