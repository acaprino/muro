# Colour Critique (measure, judge, preview)

Says what a viewer will feel, proves it with measurements and gives the exact change that fixes it: every finding carries numbers and a reason. `<CA>` below stands for `<package_root>/scripts/charles-skills/skills/design/charles-color-analysis`. The method is `<CA>/SKILL.source.md`; the reason, the numbers and the fix behind every check are in `<CA>/references/principles.md`, and the script is `<CA>/scripts/color.py`. Open a section when a step names it. Each original is the full version and wins where this body differs, except for the changes listed in the Sources section.

## Behavior

Before the first critique in a session read `<CA>/references/principles.md`: the source orders it, and its contents line lists the 13 principles, `## Mood targets`, `## Finding new palettes` and `## The colour-wheel caveat`.

1. **Route** (`<CA>/SKILL.source.md`, `## 1. Route: what is the visual for?`, with the table of the three routes). GRAPHIC stops the scroll or sells one idea at a glance; LANDING expresses a brand and then leads to one action; APP is used again and again to read, find and act; PALETTE is only colours and is judged for each route it will live in. Route first: a rule from the wrong route gives a confident, wrong critique. A hero or first screen alone runs once as GRAPHIC with its CTA checked by LANDING's rules; a full page runs as LANDING and its hero again as GRAPHIC with `--crop`. A palette with no areas assumes 60/30/10 shares by role and says so, but proportion is never judged from that assumption: lay it onto a stand-in screen (HTML, rendered with `shot`) and measure that. Then write the one line of intent: "A <route> for <what, for whom>, meant to feel <two or three mood words>; the eye should land on <the subject, offer or CTA>." The mood words come from the brief, the brand or the category; a recorded `aesthetic_direction` or `palette`, the project's `design_file` and `design_tokens` are that brief and the design's own values. With none, infer the words from the content and say they are inferred. Where the brief and the design's evident intent disagree, name both, judge against the brief and give a direction for each.
2. **Measure** with `python3 <CA>/scripts/color.py` (`## 2. Measure`: the six commands, the arguments, the roles `palette` takes and what the script only guesses). Always pass `--out` with a scratch folder. Run `--focus BOX` on every CTA and every subject you judge, rerun with `--accent` when the accent guess is wrong, and pass `--scale` so text is judged at its real size (1 for a 1× capture, a design export or a generated image; 2 retina; 3 most iPhones; 0.5 a half-size capture). Read every designed colour you quote with `sample`. For a website or web app, screenshot at 1440×900 and 390×844 and evaluate `<CA>/scripts/dom_colors.js` in the page (`window.COLOR_ROOT = "<selector>"` first to judge one component), save the string as JSON and add `--dom file.json`. Open the board and look at it. The script guesses and you decide: pixel text contrast is an estimate, off by up to 15 Lc either way and up to 30 below 1× scale, so confirm every pair you report with `contrast`.
3. **Look before judging** (`## 3. Look before judging`). Write down where the eye should land; which colours are content (photo, product, skin, food, sky: facts unless they can be graded or reshot) and which are designed (the levers); and what each designed colour claims to be: a material, a light, print ink or UI paint.
4. **Walk the eleven checks in order** (`## 4. Walk the checks in order`; each has its principle in `references/principles.md` under the same number): value, one loudest thing, 60/30/10, hue relations, pop or harmony, material and light, temperature, neutrals, families with peers and ramps, text and UI contrast, colour-blind safety and meaning. The order is deliberate: a value problem makes hue fixes pointless, and contrast failures outrank taste. Note each finding with its numbers, and apply only the rules of the visual's route.
5. **Fix** (`## 5. Fix`). Change one channel per fix, picked by the finding: move L when it cannot be read or is flat in greyscale; lower C when it is too loud or synthetic for what it claims to be; turn H 10–25° or grade the whole design 5–10% toward one colour for two lights, a wrong mood or a temperature clash; change areas, not colours, when nothing leads or the accent is too big or too small. Two channels only when the finding names two problems. Give exact values as OKLCH and hex and check them with `palette` or `contrast` before writing them down. Never fix by adding a colour, and never fix contrast by raising chroma. In GRAPHIC a weak accent is fixed by committing harder, never by toning it down; in APP take colour away before adding any. Preview the top two fixes: `recolor` for an image, CSS overrides injected into the live page and a new screenshot for web UI, the stand-in screen rendered again for a palette. When the intent is unclear or a fix costs something the design may want, give 2–3 directions, each a full measured palette, and say what each costs; the calibration against the commonest generated looks is in the same section.
6. **Write the critique** (`## 6. Write the critique`): the intent line in italics, then **Verdict** (two sentences; when the user asks whether someone else's critique is right, a point-by-point answer follows, each point with a number), **Keep** (1–3 things), **Fix** (ranked by impact, 1–6, only real problems; what stops people reading or operating comes first), **Directions** (optional) and **Numbers** (colour, role, area share, OKLCH and the contrast pairs quoted). Plain words; "complementary" only with the OKLCH gap in degrees and the wheel meant; where APCA and WCAG disagree on a pair give both and say which one you judged by and why.
7. **Check before sending** (`## 7. Check before sending`), then **record** `color_critique` (the critique) and `color_previews` (the paths of the boards and previews in the scratch folder, with what each shows; `none` with the reason when nothing could be rendered).

## Specifics

- **Floors and standards.** The text floors are APCA |Lc| by route (`references/principles.md`, `## 10. Contrast floors and the lightness-only repair`). WCAG 2 applies when a contract names it; a project design file or the user naming a WCAG level is such a contract.
- **The step changes nothing in the project.** Boards, previews and stand-in screens go to the scratch folder; the palettes under Directions are candidates. Putting a chosen value into the code or into a `DESIGN.md` is a later request.
- **What the script needs.** Python 3 with numpy and Pillow: check with `python3 -c "import numpy, PIL"`. The source writes `python3`; where that name does not start Python 3 (often on Windows), use the name the host has for it, such as `python`. The source names the two packages and gives no install command; both are published on PyPI. `shot` needs Google Chrome or Chromium: the script looks in the two macOS application folders and for `google-chrome`, `chromium`, `chromium-browser` or `chrome` on the PATH, and when it finds none it says to screenshot the page with your browser tool instead. Reading the text in an image uses macOS (Swift and Vision); without it the script reads no text lines and contrast is checked from colours you sample.
- A missing package is installed when the task needs it, said in one line (what, from PyPI, into the user's or the project's Python environment); a failed install blocks only the measurements that depend on it.

## Checks

- **route_and_intent_stated**: the critique opens with the one-line intent, names the route, and every rule applied belongs to that route.
- **colours_read_not_assumed**: every colour quoted was read with `sample` or comes from the design's own values, and every area claim comes from a measured image or stand-in, not from a hex list or an assumed share.
- **contrast_pairs_confirmed**: every contrast pair reported was confirmed with `contrast`, not read off the pixel estimate.
- **findings_have_numbers_reason_fix**: every finding has numbers, a reason and an exact fix in OKLCH and hex, or is an explicit keep; the fix list holds only real problems.
- **top_fixes_previewed**: the top two fixes have a preview (a recoloured image, a new screenshot or a stand-in rendered again), or the critique says which preview could not be made and why.
- **project_files_unchanged**: no file of the project and no design file was changed; every board and preview is in the scratch folder.
