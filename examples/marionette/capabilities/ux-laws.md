# UX Laws (design, build and review a screen or a flow)

Makes a screen or a flow show only what its tasks need, ask for nothing the product could work out, read in groups at a glance, put one thing first and end by saying it worked. `<UL>` below stands for `<package_root>/scripts/charles-skills/skills/drops/charles-design-laws`. The method is `<UL>/SKILL.source.md`: each of its twenty laws says what people do, what to build and how to check it. Open the section of a law when a step reaches it. The original is the full version and wins where this body differs, except for the changes listed in the Sources section.

## Behavior

Walk steps 1 to 8 in order, every time: a later step never rescues an earlier one. For a flow, run steps 2 to 5 on each screen, then step 6 across the whole flow. Run every check on the rendered screen at the width most people use it at (a 390 × 844 px phone when you do not know), then only the layout checks (Pareto, proximity, Von Restorff, the five-second look) at any other width it supports; judge layout and looks on the render and behaviour in the code. Skip a law nothing in scope can trigger.

1. **Name the job** (`## 1. Name the job`): the job in one sentence; the tasks, most-used first, from usage data when there is any and otherwise from the product's core task, with every inferred task marked "(assumed)" and the business's own goals ranked below what people came to do; the inventory of every element, every question asked and every step; and what is missing for each task, each missing piece a **broken** finding.
2. **Cut what does not help** (`## 2. Cut what doesn't help`): every item goes through the section's tree (no task needs it: delete; most people need it on most visits: keep in view by rank; otherwise behind one row that opens in place), then Pareto principle, Occam's razor, progressive disclosure (never more than two levels), cognitive load and the law of Prägnanz. What people need before they commit, the ways back and to help, and the business's goals always stay in view.
3. **Take work off people** (`## 3. Take work off people`): Tesler's law, with its tree for every question left (known: fill it in, shown and editable; workable out: ask only for that one thing; same answer for most and a wrong guess costs nothing: preselect; otherwise ask with a control that holds the format), the default effect (never preselect what costs money, shares data or signs people up), recognition over recall (up to 5 recents or visible choices), the paradox of the active user (no tour before the first action; teach at the point of use) and Parkinson's law (autofill hints, and a long task says how long it takes).
4. **Group what belongs together** (`## 4. Group what belongs together`): the lightest signal that shows each group, by the section's tree: proximity (the gap between groups at least twice the largest gap inside one), common region (never a region inside a region inside a region), uniform connectedness, then similarity across the screen (every role exactly one style).
5. **Point the eye at one thing** (`## 5. Point the eye at one thing`): Von Restorff effect (exactly one element per view breaks the pattern, with a second difference beside colour) and the serial position effect for hand-ordered lists.
6. **Carry people to the end** (`## 6. Carry people to the end`): goal-gradient effect (progress never reads zero once started and never goes backward), Zeigarnik effect (open items, saved progress, return where they stopped) and the peak-end rule (a success screen that says what happened, what is next and one action; fix the worst moment first).
7. **Polish last** (`## 7. Polish last`): the aesthetic-usability effect, only once steps 2 to 6 hold; never let polish pass a screen they failed.
8. **Verify** (`## 8. Verify`): five seconds, new person, leave and return. When the screen cannot be rendered, do not build an app to render it: list the checks you could not run and what each would catch.
9. **Build** (`ui.ux-laws`): a failed check means fix it and check again. Anything the request asked for gets built even when a law argues against it: build it, and name the law it breaks in what you deliver. Deliver as `# Deliver`, "Building", says.
10. **Review** (`ui.ux-laws-review`): change nothing and walk the same steps. Every failed check and every broken Build rule is a finding, written once under the law whose rule names it; later steps skip what an earlier finding removes. Report as `# Deliver`, "Reviewing", says: the job and tasks, what is missing, one line on the flow's worst moment, the table (Severity, Law, Where, Before, After) sorted by severity, the problems no law covers, the checks you could not run.
11. **Record** `ux_changes` (files and what changed, one line per law that changed something; `none` for a review) and `ux_report` (the review, or for a build the checks run with their results and those not run).

## Specifics

- **Severity** (`# Deliver`): **broken** means a task fails or ends wrong (something it needs is missing, people cannot find the primary action, pick the wrong thing, lose what they entered, or pay or share data through a default they did not choose); **friction** means it works but is slower, harder or more confusing than it needs to be; **polish** is only the looks.
- **A recorded system is deliberate.** Values that trace to a project `DESIGN.md` that is not a starter or a reference, to a design-system Master or tokens in the repository, to a recorded `palette`, `typography`, `aesthetic_direction` or `constraints`, or to the client's brand are not changed here: where step 7 or a Build rule would change one (the accent colour, a radius, a token), report it. The design file is never rewritten in this step (`design-file` owns it).
- **Outside these twenty laws.** The source leaves motion, target sizes, how many choices to offer, chunking, conventions, messy input and response times to another skill of its repository, which in this package is the capability `interaction-motion`. A problem there goes in the list of problems no law covers, with that name beside it; this step applies none of its rules.
- **Beside design guidance.** A recorded `design_candidates` or `stack_guidance` is a set of proposals, not a decision. In the lookup rules read at harmonization none contradicts a law; where a recorded proposal does differ from a law's rule, follow this method and name the difference in the report.
- Nothing is installed.

## Checks

- **job_and_tasks_written**: the job, the ranked tasks with assumed ones marked, the inventory and what is missing were written before any change or finding.
- **steps_walked_in_order**: steps 2 to 7 were walked in order, each law's check answered or the law skipped because nothing in scope can trigger it.
- **checks_run_on_render**: layout and looks were judged on the rendered screen at the stated width and behaviour in the code, or the render checks are listed as not run.
- **requested_breaks_named**: everything the request asked for was built, and each law it breaks is named with why it matters.
- **recorded_system_respected**: no value of a recorded system, project design file, Master or client brand was changed; what conflicts with it is reported.
- **findings_in_source_table**: the review opens with the job, the tasks, what is missing and the worst moment, and every fix is a row with severity, law, where, before and after; no file was changed.
- **unrun_checks_listed**: every check that could not be run is listed with what it would catch.
