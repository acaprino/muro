# Interaction Motion (build and review how an interface moves and responds)

Every interaction answers five questions in this order: should it move, where should the eye go, how should it move, can people find it, hit it and choose it, and can they finish. A later answer never rescues an earlier one. `<UA>` below stands for `<package_root>/scripts/charles-skills/skills/drops/charles-ux-animation`. The method is `<UA>/SKILL.source.md`; exact values for common components are in `<UA>/references/recipes.md` and the techniques in `<UA>/references/techniques.md`. Both references open with a list of their sections: read the section a step points to. Each original is the full version and wins where this body differs, except for the changes listed in the Sources section.

## Behavior

1. **Match the stack** (`## Run it`). Find what the project already animates with (CSS transitions, Tailwind, Motion as `motion` or `framer-motion`, React Spring, WAAPI) and write in that. State changes stay in CSS transitions. If Motion is installed, use it only for what CSS cannot do: animating an element out as it unmounts, layout changes, and springs that follow a finger. Never add a library for what a CSS transition does, and never hand-roll a spring where Motion is installed.
2. **List the interactions** in scope: what triggers each (pointer, touch, keyboard, network, scroll), how often one person fires it, and what it has to tell them. Then cut, or in a review flag, every element and step that does not serve the task before polishing anything.
3. **Should it move?** (`## 1. Should it move?`). The frequency tree: many times a session or from the keyboard means no transition; a few times a session means animate within the timing table; rarely may be choreographed, each move up to 500ms and the whole sequence up to about 2.5s. Motion that answers no question is cut. Press and hover feedback keeps its 100–150ms at every frequency. Nothing already at rest when the page first paints plays an entrance. Then the Doherty threshold: the pressed state on the same frame as the input; optimistic updates for actions that almost never fail; a spinner or "Saving…" only after 300ms and held at least 500ms once shown; a skeleton past 1s where the result appears.
4. **Where should the eye go?** (`## 2. Where should the eye go?`): staging (one primary motion at a time), stagger (copies 30–50ms apart, only the first 6; distinct pieces 80–100ms apart), Von Restorff, serial position, secondary action and exaggeration, each with its limits.
5. **How should it move?** (`## 3. How should it move?`): the timing table (press, hover, colour 100–150ms; tooltip 125ms; dropdown, popover 150–250ms; modal, drawer, sheet, toast 200–300ms; moving or resizing in place 200–300ms; page transition and shared-element move 300–400ms). Exits are 20–30% shorter than their entrance, and quieter. Curves by the tree of `### Slow in and slow out` (a spring for what follows a finger, with bounce 0; ease-out `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)` to appear; ease-in to leave; ease-in-out on screen; linear only where the motion is time itself), never ease-in for an entrance, never `transition: all`. Then origin (scale from the trigger, from `scale(0.95)` with `opacity: 0`, never `scale(0)`), arcs, anticipation and the press (`scale(0.96)` on `:active`, never below 0.95; hover only under `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`), follow through, squash and stretch, pose to pose, solid drawing, meaning, replacements, interruptibility, and compound motion and loops, each applied only when its subsection says it applies. For a component a recipe covers, open its section of `references/recipes.md`; for a technique, its section of `references/techniques.md`.
6. **Can people find it, hit it and choose it?** (`## 4. Can people find it, hit it and choose it?`): proximity, similarity, uniform connectedness, Prägnanz, Fitts's law (targets at least 44×44px on touch and 40×40px with a pointer; Cancel takes the default focus in a destructive confirmation), the hit area recipe (never below 24×24px where two zones would overlap), Hick's law (groups of 7 or fewer; never pre-select anything that costs money or shares data), Miller's law and Jakob's law.
7. **Can people finish?** (`## 5. Can people finish?`): Tesler's law, Parkinson's law, Occam's razor with progressive disclosure, the Pareto principle, Postel's law (parse every common form; validate on blur or submit, never while a field is first being typed), errors (prevent, then recover: anything reversible gets Undo for at least 5 seconds instead of a confirmation; confirm only destructive actions that cannot be undone), Zeigarnik with goal gradient, and the peak-end rule.
8. **Name the numbers.** Durations, curves and springs live in named tokens at the top of the file or in the theme, and every animation uses them. A sequence of three or more stages gets a storyboard (`references/techniques.md`, `## Storyboards`). When the app can be run, tune the values live, then hard-code what works.
9. **Verify** with the eleven checks of `## Verify` (scrub at 10% speed, interrupt, hammer the keyboard, slow the network, tap the edges, paste messy input and force each failure, finish every flow, reduced motion, switch the theme, animated properties, the tenth use). In a code-only review do not build a harness: after the findings, list the checks you could not run and what each would catch.
10. **Review** (`ui.interaction-review`): change nothing. Open with the interaction list, then one table row per finding: severity, principle, where (file and line, or frame), before, after. Severity is **broken** (a wrong result, lost input, a blocked or skipped step), **friction** or **polish**; sort by severity, then by the five questions. Bugs no principle covers go in a list after the table.
11. **Record** `interaction_changes` (files and what changed, with the tokens added; `none` for a review) and `interaction_report` (the review, or for a build the checks run with their results, those not run and what was reported instead of changed).

## Specifics

- **Recorded motion comes first.** The confirmed motion values of a project `DESIGN.md` that is not a starter or a reference, then a recorded `motion-spec` and `reduced-motion`, decide durations, curves and reduced-motion alternatives where they differ from this method's table and trees; a Master or recorded `constraints` that limit motion do the same. With none recorded, the source's values apply as written. The design file is never rewritten in this step (`design-file` owns it).
- **Reduced motion.** The source turns every animation off under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, fades included, with the same end states and information; the one exception is an indicator that work is still happening. A recorded `reduced-motion` that keeps opacity fades is followed instead, and the report says so.
- **Beside design guidance.** A recorded `design_candidates` or `stack_guidance` is a set of proposals. Where the lookup data and this method agree on a value, use it: exits 30% shorter than entrances satisfy both, as does a press scale of 0.96, and 48dp on Android satisfies both target minimums. Where they differ with no common value (the lookup data prefers spring curves and confirms before destructive actions; this method keeps springs for what follows a finger and gives Undo to what is reversible), follow this method and name the difference in the report.
- **Scroll.** Scroll-linked animation is the scroll-code step's. This step animates what people trigger.
- Nothing is installed: the method adds no library the project does not have.

## Checks

- **interactions_listed_first**: every interaction in scope was listed with its trigger, its frequency and what it has to tell people, before any change or finding.
- **questions_walked_in_order**: the five questions were walked in order for every interaction, each subsection applied or skipped because nothing in scope can trigger it.
- **numbers_in_named_tokens**: every duration, curve and spring the step wrote is a named token, and a sequence of three or more stages has its storyboard.
- **stack_matched**: the animations are written in what the project already animates with; no library was added for what a CSS transition does.
- **recorded_motion_respected**: no value of a recorded motion specification, project design file or Master was contradicted; where the method differs, the recorded value was used and the difference reported.
- **reduced_motion_holds**: under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` every end state and every piece of information is the same as with motion on, by the source's rule or the recorded alternative.
- **findings_in_source_table**: the review opens with the interaction list and every finding is a row with severity, principle, where, before and after; no file was changed.
- **unrun_checks_listed**: every verification check that could not be run is listed with what it would catch.
