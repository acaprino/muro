# Visual Direction (landing page and website)

Plans, writes, builds and checks a landing page, a marketing site or a company website page the way the pinned landing-page-design does: its Part A decides what the page says and how it is structured, its Part B is the visual system every visual value resolves through, and the page is built section by section. `<LP>` below stands for `<package_root>/scripts/ai-design-skills/skills/landing-page-design`.

## Behavior

`<LP>/SKILL.source.md` is the full version of this method. Open the section a step names when the task reaches it. Where this body and the original differ, the original wins, except for the changes listed under Sources.

0. **Scope by the selected intent.** Landing strategy alone stops after step 3 and opens only Part A, "B8. Content realism" and "Output format". A direction without a build stops after step 4. Record every output not produced as `not requested: <reason>`.
1. **Read what exists.** The project's stack and the pages it already has, the brief's material (logo, screens, photographs, prices, proof) and a project `DESIGN.md` (see Specifics). "A landing page is not a homepage": a landing page wins one intent, one offer, one audience, one primary action; a company website page that serves several intents keeps the visual system of step 4 and takes from Part A what its content needs.
2. **Intake** ("A1. Intake"). Gather purpose, audience and context, proof and assets, and constraints before designing or writing. Ask only for what is missing, and ask in one batch rather than one question at a time. If the user cannot answer, make a reasonable assumption, state it in one line, and continue. Do not stall the build.
3. **Strategy and copy, before any code.** Return, in order, the seven parts of "Output format" with the counts it gives: page outline, hero copy, benefits, how it works, FAQ, SEO/AEO (index or noindex by "A7. SEO and AEO") and the layout recommendation (one of A to D by "A3. Layout selection", and why). The outline follows "A2. Page structure", the mandatory tagline reveal section of B11 included. From "A4. Conversion rules": one primary CTA, never competing CTAs above the fold; at least one risk reducer (free trial, free plan, no credit card, cancel anytime, money back guarantee); move the FAQ earlier for high friction offers. Put proof directly beside the claim it supports. The final CTA is identical to the top one. Open A4, A5 and A8 for the remaining conversion rules, the copy formulas and the pitfalls; the copy rules of B1 apply (no hyphens inside text, no orphaned words). From "B8. Content realism": on a real product's page prices, guarantees, statistics, customer counts, testimonials and logos come from the user or a real source, and a missing fact is a clearly marked placeholder such as `{{price}}` or `{{testimonial}}`; invented people, brands and sample numbers belong to fictional demos and prototypes only.
4. **Fix the visual system** ("PART B — Visual system": every visual value must resolve through these rules instead of being invented ad hoc). Open B1 to B7 before any code and write on the plan what the page uses:
   - **Typeface** (B1): one of Geist, Manrope, Geist Mono, Poppins; never Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans, Helvetica; one typeface per site, a pair only when the prompt explicitly asks for it (Geist Mono beside the main font only for code, data or numeric UI); never italic, never 900 / black.
   - **Text styles and scale** (B1): every piece of text uses one of the named styles (Heading, Title, Body, Button, Caption), every size lands on Tailwind's default type scale, an off-scale size snaps to the closest step below with its paired line height. Define each style once as a class or token in kebab case.
   - **Spacing and radius** (B2, B3): only the thirteen spacing values of the table; Tailwind's radius values, with the nested radius formula where the gap is under 32px.
   - **Colour and backgrounds** (B4, B5): flat backgrounds, never a gradient; in dark mode only the six listed background colours; never a border on only one side of a card; the hero heading's left to right text gradient is the one gradient.
   - **Icons** (B6): Phosphor, Solar or Iconamoon; never Material Icons or Material Symbols.
   - **Motion** (B7): the base curve and its durations, only `transform` and `opacity`, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected; the navigation and the scroll reveals as B7 describes them.
5. **Build section by section** in the order of "A6. Build order": hero, benefits, how it works, proof, FAQ, final CTA. Never rebuild the whole page on each iteration. The hero follows "B5. Hero section" (heading and subheading capped at 680px, line breaks where the thought breaks). The tagline reveal section follows "B11. Tagline reveal section (mandatory)": at least two lines, its own moment further down the page, words activating one at a time. Write real copy and system messages by B8, and give every interactive element the full state set of "B9. States": hover, active, focus, loading, empty, error; no dead links.
6. **Ship check.** Go through "B10. Ship requirements" item by item, the check at 390px wide included (no horizontal overflow, buttons keep their text inside, the primary action visible in the first screen), then the three blocks of "Quick checklist" line by line. Fix what fails.
7. **Report and record.** Report what was built, every assumption of step 2 with its one line, every placeholder left for a missing fact, every deviation from a rule with the instruction that asked for it, and what could not be checked. Then record for the handoff below:
   - `offer`, `page_outline`: the strategy and the outline of step 3 (for a page that is not a landing page, its main action and its sections).
   - `palette`: the theme, the background and text colours, the accent and where each came from (the user, the brand's material, a stated assumption).
   - `typography`: the typeface and the named text styles in use.
   - `layout`: the layout type of A3 and the sections in their order.
   - `constraints`: the rules later steps must keep, including the motion rules of B7 as applied, and any deviation from the source with its reason.
   - `page`: the files written, or `not requested`.

## Specifics

- **The user's explicit prompt wins** over a rule of the source ("Scope": "When the user's explicit prompt conflicts with a rule, the user wins"): follow it and record the deviation and its reason in `constraints`. A framework default never does ("When a rule here conflicts with a framework default, this file wins").
- **A project design file is such an instruction.** A `DESIGN.md` of the project that is not a starter or a reference (the recorded `design_file`, or the file found in the project) counts as the user's explicit instruction for the values it confirms: its confirmed colour tokens, typefaces, spacing, radius and component values are followed where a rule of the source says otherwise. Each deviation is recorded in `constraints`. Its `unresolved` entries and `proposed` values are not instructions, and where the file is silent the method decides. This step never rewrites `DESIGN.md`: a value the page needs and the file lacks is asked of the user, and the change belongs to `design-file` in its own step. A request in the turn that contradicts the file is followed and said to do so. Where the project runs design code checks (`design-check.config.json`), source code in the checked paths with a colour value, a colour function such as `rgb()` or `oklch()`, a utility palette class, a font family or a `must-avoid` effect the file does not permit fails them: use the project's tokens there, or say that the check will fail.
- **One owner of the look and of the page.** This step owns palette, typography and layout for the page it builds. Later steps consume these outputs and do not restyle independently. Recorded `design_candidates` of `design-guidance` are candidates: Part B decides typeface, scale, spacing and backgrounds.

## Checks

- **single_primary_action**: one offer, one audience and one primary action are explicit, with no competing CTA above the fold; the final CTA is identical to the top one.
- **proof_supported**: on a real product's page every price, guarantee, statistic, customer count, testimonial and logo comes from the user or a real source, or is a clearly marked placeholder; proof sits beside the claim it supports. Invented names, brands and sample numbers appear only in a fictional demo or prototype.
- **palette_applied**: backgrounds are flat, dark mode backgrounds come from the six listed colours and the hero heading gradient is the only gradient, or the confirmed values of a project design file with each deviation recorded in `constraints`.
- **typography_consistent**: one approved typeface, every text element on a named text style and a Tailwind scale step, no italics and no ultra bold, or from the confirmed typography of a project design file.
- **visual_system_met**: the "Visual" block of the source's "Quick checklist" holds on the page line by line, or each deviation is recorded in `constraints` with the user's explicit instruction.
- **states_and_ship_met**: the "Content and ship" block of the "Quick checklist" holds: real draft copy, the six states, no dead links and the current nav item indicated, 404, legal links, form validation, favicon, meta tags and alt text.
- **mobile_checked**: the page was opened at 390px wide: no horizontal overflow, buttons keep their text inside and the primary action is visible in the first screen. A page that could not be opened at that width makes this not-tested, with the reason.
